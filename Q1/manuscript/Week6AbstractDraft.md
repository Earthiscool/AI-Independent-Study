Abstract

Dropout is a widely used technique for reducing overfitting in neural networks, but it is usually evaluated only by its effect on overall accuracy. This study investigated how dropout affects both accuracy and the mistakes a network makes between visually similar clothing categories in Fashion-MNIST, and how these effects change with the amount of training data. A multilayer perceptron with two hidden layers was trained with dropout rates of 0.0, 0.2, and 0.5 on 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, and 10,000 images, using 10 random seeds per condition (120 runs). Models were trained with early stopping, evaluated once on the official test set, and compared using paired t-tests with Holm correction. This design followed a 10-epoch pilot study in which dropout showed no benefit. Dropout 0.5 increased test accuracy by 0.53 to 0.95 percentage points, a statistically significant improvement at all four training sizes, and 7 of 8 dropout comparisons were significant. Dropout made results more consistent across seeds and, in exploratory analyses, strongly reduced overfitting. It lowered confusion among T-shirts, pullovers, coats, and shirts by about one percentage point, but at the same rate as other errors, and shirt remained the hardest class. The gain from dropout was smaller with 10,000 images, but this difference was not significant. Dropout gives a small but consistent benefit when networks are trained long enough to overfit, but it does not specifically help distinguish similar categories. Experiments that stop training too early may miss its effect entirely.

Keywords: dropout, regularization, overfitting, Fashion-MNIST, multilayer perceptron, training set size, error analysis
